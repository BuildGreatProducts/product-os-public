# Pricing Strategy Framework

A worksheet for putting one honest price on your product — enough to make a real ask this week. Fill in each section below in 1-2 sentences max.

This is deliberately a first pass, not the full monetization model. It gives your Mini-Launch (`4-Mini-Launch.md`) a price to carry. The full pricing ladder, cost & margin, and revenue-model work is an optional deep dive (`BONUS-Business-Strategy-Deep-Dive.md`) for when the money questions get real — typically before spending on paid channels.

---

## 1. Who Pays

*Who actually hands over the money — and out of which budget?*

> Good: a named buyer and a budget bucket — "solo consultant, personal software budget under $100/mo", "head of ops, team-tooling budget, needs no sign-off under $50/seat"
> Bad: "everyone", "users", a buyer who is a different person from the user with no note on how that gets bridged

## 2. Launch Price

*One price you would say out loud to a stranger this week.*

> Good: one number with a billing shape that matches the value — "$29/month", "$199 one-time", "$500 for a 4-week pilot"
> Bad: a range you'd negotiate down from, "free for now, we'll monetize later", a price you have never said out loud to anyone

**Common shapes — pick one:**

| Shape | When it fits |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly subscription | Continuous value — the product is better every month they keep it |
| One-time purchase | Bounded utility — the job gets done and stays done |
| Pilot / founding-member fee | Services, B2B, or anything you'll deliver partly by hand at first |
| Founding-user deal | Early believers get a locked-in low price in exchange for feedback |

## 3. Price Anchors

*Which 2–3 products does your customer already pay for that set their expectation of what this should cost?*

> Good: named products with today's real prices — "Notion $12/seat, Calendly $12/mo — we sit just above, at $19, because we replace both"
> Bad: no anchors, anchoring against free tools, anchors you haven't checked the current price of

## 4. Your Price Line

*The one plain-English sentence that carries the price into your Mini-Launch.*

> Good: "I'm building X for Y — it'll be around $29/month, about half what [anchor] charges. Would that be worth it for you?"
> Bad: a paragraph of hedging, apologizing for the price, hiding the number behind "affordable"

Including the price in your mini-launch message is **optional — but recommended.** A post with a price gets you a dramatically more honest signal than one without: "sounds cool" is free, "I'd pay $29 for that" is data. If saying the number out loud feels scary, that's normal — it's also exactly the fear the mini-launch is designed to break.
