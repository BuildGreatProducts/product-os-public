# BONUS - Growth Experiments Library

*A bonus asset for ProductOS — a menu of proven, channel-by-channel growth experiments, each one a small, falsifiable bet you can run in a week to make a chosen channel work harder.*

`productos/distribute/1-Go-To-Market-Strategy.md` picks your channels and gets you started. This library is what you reach for next: once a channel is live, you stop guessing and start running **experiments** — small changes with a clear hypothesis, a pass threshold, and a decision at the end. Pick from the menu below, tailor each to your product, and log every result in `productos/distribute/3-Growth-Experiments-Tracker.md`.

> **The meta-rule:** An experiment is a falsifiable bet, not a chore. It names what you'll change, what you expect to happen, the number that means you were right, and what you'll do either way. "Post more" is not an experiment. "Five hook variations, one a day; a winner clears 50% 3-second retention" is.

---

## The method

**The loop.** Every experiment runs the same five beats: **Hypothesis → run small → measure against a threshold → decide → log.** The decision is always one of three: **double down** (it beat the threshold — do more), **iterate** (close — change one thing and rerun), or **kill** (it missed — stop and try a different experiment). Then log it in `productos/distribute/3-Growth-Experiments-Tracker.md` so the learning compounds.

**Prioritize by effort and impact.** Don't rate experiments with a formula. Rate each on two plain axes — **Effort** (Low / Medium / High: time and money to run) and **Impact** (Low / Medium / High: how much it could move your metric) — then sequence:

| | Low effort | High effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High impact** | ⭐ Quick wins — do these first | Big bets — do these next, one at a time |
| **Low impact** | Filler — only when you're blocked | Skip — these are time sinks |

Always clear the quick wins before the big bets. A first-time builder should never be running a high-effort, low-impact experiment.

**Run a tight cadence.** One to three experiments at a time, on **one** focus channel, in weekly or two-week cycles. More than three in flight and you can't tell what moved the number. Same discipline as the rest of the system: one variable, one threshold, one decision.

**Measure what counts.** A result is a number that ladders to your north star — signups, trials, paying users, booked calls, cost-per-install. Not views, not likes, not followers. If an experiment's "win" is a vanity metric, redesign it until the win is revenue-shaped.

---

## How to read a card

Each experiment below follows the same shape — a **Hypothesis** (what you'll change and expect), **The test** (the design), **Pass =** (the number and timeframe that means it worked), **Next steps** (the simple actions to set it up), a **Decision** (what to do with the result), and a **Real example** (a founder who ran a version of it). The hypotheses use [brackets] for you to fill in — tailor every one to your product and your customer's own words before you run it.

## 1. Short-form content

*TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts — the discovery engine. Win on the hook, the format, and volume; short-form's job is reach and testing what resonates, then funnelling viewers to your product and your long-form.*

#### Hook Test
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** High · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Leading the first 2–3 seconds of [your demo] with [the customer's pain] instead of the product will lift 3-second retention and saves.

**The test:** post 5 versions of the same clip — identical body, different opening hook — one a day.

**Pass =** one hook clears 50% 3-second retention and 2× your median saves across the 5 posts, within 5 days.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick your best 15–30 second demo clip.
2. Write 5 opening lines in your customer's words (a pain, a result, a "POV…").
3. Schedule one a day; after 5 days compare retention + saves.

**Decision:** winner becomes your default opener — rerun with 5 fresh hooks. Miss all 5 → it's the format, not the hook (try Format Clone).

> *Real example — Mal Baron, Prayer Lock, $20K/mo: recorded ~40 takes until one hook format clicked.*

#### Format Clone
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** High · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Rebuilding [a proven viral format in our niche] with our product will reach the audience that already engages with it.

**The test:** find the top viral video in your niche, copy its structure (hook, beats, CTA), remake it with your product.

**Pass =** your version clears 10K organic views with no paid boost, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Search your niche hashtag; sort by most-liked.
2. Note the top video's hook, length, captions, and CTA.
3. Remake it with your demo; post; clone the next format if it lands.

**Decision:** hit → make 3 more in that format. Miss → clone a different proven format, not a fresh idea.

> *Real example — Steven Cravada, PuffCount, $40K/mo: copied a 3.7M-like "vape-in-water" format; organic TikTok hit 50M+ views.*

#### Cadence Ramp
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** Going from [current volume] to 3+ posts a day will find the cadence where the algorithm starts compounding.

**The test:** hold format constant, triple posting volume for 2 weeks.

**Pass =** at least one breakout post (5× your median views) and a rising 7-day view trend.

**Next steps:**
1. Batch-film a week of clips in one sitting.
2. Schedule 3 a day.
3. Track median and best views weekly.

**Decision:** trend up → hold the cadence. Flat → volume isn't the lever; return to hooks and formats.

> *Real example — Uray (Yuri), Rummer, $300K in 2024: 200M+ organic short-form views by posting at volume.*

## 2. Long-form content

*YouTube, podcasts, newsletters, long threads and essays — the depth engine. Build each asset around one search-intent, answer fast, and let it compound; this is where authority and trust are built. Feed it with the hooks that won in short-form.*

#### Teach-the-Pain Tutorial
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** A focused how-to video answering [one specific customer question], built around that exact search, will pull in people with precisely that problem and convert a few each — and keep doing it as a searchable asset.

**The test:** record one tutorial that solves a single customer pain, built around one search query — answer fast, cut the long intro, show your product as the natural "how."

**Pass =** one problem-specific video drives 3+ paying customers (or [N] qualified signups) within 90 days, even at a few hundred views.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick the question customers ask you most.
2. Record a short, focused walkthrough that answers it fast (no long intro).
3. Title and describe it for the exact search phrase; link the product below.
4. Track signups per video; make the next one for the next-most-asked question.

**Decision:** a video that converts → make it a weekly series, one search-intent each. Views but no signups → the path to product is the gap, not the topic.

> *Real example — Vikash, Bulk Mockup, $12K/mo: a YouTube "content flywheel" of narrow how-to videos; a 370-view video drove $345 MRR from 3 customers.*

#### Creator Collab
**Effort:** High · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** A long-form video made with [a creator whose audience is our customer] will borrow trust we can't build alone and send qualified viewers.

**The test:** collaborate on (or sponsor a genuine segment in) one long-form video from a creator whose audience is your customer.

**Pass =** the collab drives [N] signups at a cost-per-acquisition under payback, within 30 days of going live.

**Next steps:**
1. List creators whose long-form audience is your exact customer.
2. Pitch a genuinely useful collab or segment, not a generic ad read.
3. Give them a unique link or code; track signups.
4. Re-run with the best-performing creator; find lookalikes.

**Decision:** profitable → line up more collabs like it. Flat → audience mismatch; re-pick the creator, not the format.

> *Real example — Lane Wagner, boot.dev, ~$1M/mo: Free Code Camp YouTube collabs and creator marketing (incl. gaming audiences) scaled it to 25,000+ paying members.*

#### Long-Form Story Post
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** Medium · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** A long-form written story — a build, a result, or a lesson — with the product woven in will out-convert a plain announcement on [X / LinkedIn / your newsletter].

**The test:** publish one long-form post (a thread, essay, or newsletter) telling a specific story, ending with a soft product mention and link; hold a consistent weekly cadence.

**Pass =** one post drives [N] signups (or a clear click-through spike) within a week.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick a specific story with a number or a lesson (a before/after, a milestone, a mistake).
2. Write it in your customer's words; end with a soft product mention + link.
3. Post on your strongest written platform; reply to every comment.
4. Repeat weekly; keep the angles that drive clicks.

**Decision:** converts → lock a weekly cadence. Flat → sharpen the story to a more specific, concrete result.

> *Real example — Sajjad (CJ), CodeGuide.dev, $42K/mo: a fixed cadence of ~4 threads + 3 long-form posts a week on X built the audience and the revenue.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools** ([BONUS - AI Distribution Tools](BONUS-AI-Distribution-Tools.md) §1–2, §7): **Descript** to record/edit video and podcasts, **Surfer**/**Frase** to build each video around one search intent (Teach-the-Pain Tutorial), **Taplio**/**Hypefury** for long-form threads and **Beehiiv** for a newsletter (Long-Form Story Post).
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** the **Notion** MCP to run the content calendar and draft scripts; **Claude in Chrome** + **web search** to find the exact search intent and the right collab creators; the **Figma** plugin for thumbnails. Check the registry for a YouTube/analytics MCP to track watch-time → signups.

## 3. Communities

#### Framing Test
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** Medium · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** A personal-story framing ("I built this because…") will outperform a feature announcement in [your communities].

**The test:** same demo, two framings, posted in two comparable communities.

**Pass =** the story framing drives 2× the link clicks (UTM) and stays up (not removed), within 1 week.

**Next steps:**
1. Write both versions.
2. Post one in each of two similar communities.
3. Compare UTM clicks and sentiment.

**Decision:** winner becomes your template everywhere. Both removed → you're posting like an ad; go value-first.

> *Real example — Ovnish, Save Wise, $25K/mo: matched the group's exact vocabulary before posting; Reddit + Facebook groups carried the business.*

#### Value-First Thread
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** Answering [the niche's most-repeated question] with a genuinely useful post, product linked softly, will convert better than a demo.

**The test:** write the definitive answer to a recurring question; mention your product only as the "how."

**Pass =** one post drives 50+ trials (or 20+ paying) from a single community, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Find the question asked weekly in your community.
2. Write the best answer in the thread — no hard pitch.
3. Link your product as one step; reply to every comment.

**Decision:** converts → turn it into a repeatable series. Flat → buyers may be elsewhere; test a new community.

> *Real example — Julien Nahum, Note Forms, $37K/mo: seeded Notion subreddits and Facebook groups with helpful posts; first wave went viral.*

#### New-Community Expansion
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** There's a higher-converting community than the one we started in.

**The test:** post a working format in one new named community per day for 2 weeks; track conversion per community.

**Pass =** find one community converting 2× your current best, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. List 10–14 named communities.
2. Post your best framing in a new one each day.
3. Rank them by trials or paying users.

**Decision:** double down on the top 1–2; drop the rest. None convert → it's a framing or product issue, not reach.

> *Real example — Diego, App Alchemy, $17K MRR: Reddit organic across many subreddits, zero ad spend.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools** ([BONUS - AI Distribution Tools](BONUS-AI-Distribution-Tools.md) §1): **Syncly** for social listening across communities; otherwise this channel is mostly hands-on.
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** **Claude in Chrome** to monitor and post in subreddits, Discords, and Facebook groups (most have no API); the **Notion** MCP to track which communities convert. Check the connector registry for a Reddit or Discord MCP.

## 4. Search (SEO & GEO)

#### Free-Tool Wedge
**Effort:** High · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** An ungated free tool targeting [a high-intent keyword] will rank and feed signups into the paid product.

**The test:** ship one single-purpose free tool (calculator, converter, generator) with no signup gate, SEO-targeted.

**Pass =** ranks top-3 for its keyword and converts 15%+ of visitors to signup, within 3 months.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick the highest-intent adjacent keyword.
2. Build the smallest useful free tool for it.
3. Add a soft path into the paid product; track rank and conversion.

**Decision:** ranks + converts → build the next free tool. Ranks but no conversion → fix the path to paid, not the tool.

> *Real example — Joseph, Super Demo, $250K/mo: dozens of ungated free tools now drive ~20% of all traffic.*

#### Comparison Page
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** A "[best X for Y]" or "[competitor] alternative" page will capture buyers at the moment of intent.

**The test:** publish one comparison/alternative page for your highest-intent buying query.

**Pass =** the page reaches page 1 and drives 10+ trials a month, within 3 months.

**Next steps:**
1. Find the buying-intent query (search "[category] alternative").
2. Write an honest comparison that includes your product.
3. Track ranking and trials.

**Decision:** ranks + converts → write the next comparison. No rank → target a lower-competition query.

> *Real example — Angus Cheng, BankStatementConverter.com, $40K MRR: ranked for exactly what buyers search; ~40K visits/mo of pure intent.*

#### AI-Answer (GEO) Pass
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** Publishing the content AI engines cite for [your category question] will get us named in ChatGPT/Perplexity answers.

**The test:** create the "best tools for [job]" content the engines pull from; re-ask the question weekly.

**Pass =** your brand named in AI answers for the core category query, within 6 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity your category question; note who's cited.
2. Publish the comparison/listicle content they favor.
3. Re-check weekly.

**Decision:** named → expand to adjacent queries. Absent → get cited on the third-party sites the engines already quote.

> *Real example — Sebastian Ghiorghiu: counts "ChatGPT mentions" as a live, tracked acquisition channel.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools** ([BONUS - AI Distribution Tools](BONUS-AI-Distribution-Tools.md) §2–3): **Surfer**/**Frase**/**Writesonic** for on-page content (Comparison Page), **Rankai** for an autonomous SEO/GEO agent, **Otterly**/**Profound** + free **HubSpot AI Search Grader** for AI-answer visibility (AI-Answer Pass).
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** **Claude in Chrome** + **web search** for keyword/SERP research and to check whether AI engines cite you (GEO); the **Notion** MCP for the content plan. Check the registry for a Search Console / analytics MCP to track rankings and traffic.

## 5. Platform ecosystems

#### Listing Optimization (ASO)
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** High · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Rewriting our [store/marketplace] title, keywords, and first screenshots will lift listing-to-install rate.

**The test:** change the title/keyword field and first 3 screenshots; compare conversion before and after.

**Pass =** 20%+ lift in listing conversion (or keyword ranking) within 3 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Put your top keyword in the title.
2. Make the first screenshot show the magic moment.
3. Compare conversion to the prior 3 weeks.

**Decision:** lift → test the next keyword cluster. Flat → ranking, not the listing, is the constraint.

> *Real example — John MacAvoy, Migo, $30K/mo: ASO on location keywords + localization scaled to 5M+ downloads.*

#### Integration Wedge
**Effort:** High · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** A native integration with [a platform our customer lives in] will put us in front of its users.

**The test:** build and ship one integration and get listed in that platform's directory.

**Pass =** the integration drives 25+ signups a month from the platform, within 6 weeks of listing.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick the one platform your customer uses daily.
2. Build the smallest useful connector.
3. List it; optimize the directory entry.

**Decision:** drives signups → build the next integration. Flat → wrong platform or weak listing; revisit.

> *Real example — Dmytro, Screenshot One, $12K/mo: Zapier/Make integrations put it inside tools users already ran.*

#### Review-Velocity Push
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** Getting our first 10–25 reviews fast will trigger the marketplace's ranking algorithm.

**The test:** ask every happy early user for a review in a 2-week push.

**Pass =** a 4.7+ rating with 20+ reviews and a measurable ranking bump, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Add an in-product prompt at the magic moment.
2. DM your first users personally.
3. Watch rating and rank.

**Decision:** rank rises → keep the prompt permanent. No movement → reviews aren't the lever here.

> *Real example — Ethan, Cut Coach, $20K/mo: a high rating and community sharing peaked during wrestling season.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools:** platform-native — your store or marketplace's own analytics and keyword fields. (The tool list focuses on the open web; app-store/marketplace optimization is done in the platform itself.)
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** the **Figma** plugin for listing screenshots; **Claude in Chrome** to research competitor listings and keywords. Check the connector registry for an MCP for the platform you build on (e.g., your marketplace) and an app-store/analytics MCP.

## 6. Launch platforms

#### Lifetime-Deal Probe
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** A 3-day lifetime deal with three tiers will produce a cash burst and a cohort of evangelists.

**The test:** run an LTD (AppSumo/RocketHub or your own) with the middle tier engineered as the default.

**Pass =** $10K+ in card swipes in the window, with 20+ buyers active weekly afterward.

**Next steps:**
1. Set 3 tiers; make the middle the obvious pick.
2. Line up the listing and first reviews.
3. Run 3 days; onboard buyers personally.

**Decision:** hits → keep the cohort as reviewers and referrers. Misses → the offer or audience is off, not the channel.

> *Real example — Devin, Supergrow, $230K/yr: a RocketHub LTD at $79/$199/$299 made $65K in 3 days and kept ~250 evangelists.*

#### Product Hunt / HN Launch
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** A coordinated launch on [Product Hunt or Hacker News] will deliver a traffic and backlink spike plus first reviews.

**The test:** prep assets and first comments, notify your small audience, launch on the platform that matches your buyer.

**Pass =** top-5 of the day (PH) or front page (HN), netting 200+ signups and durable backlinks.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick the platform that fits your buyer.
2. Prep a demo video, copy, and your first comment.
3. Rally early momentum in the first hour.

**Decision:** lands → reuse the asset kit for the next milestone. Flops → you launched to an empty room; build the audience first.

> *Real example — Jonathan, ChartDB, $9K MRR: a Hacker News launch + GitHub (21K+ stars) seeded the user base.*

#### Milestone Re-Launch
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** Medium · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Re-announcing on [a new feature or milestone] to a warmed audience will convert better than the first launch.

**The test:** ship a notable feature; relaunch it as an event to your list and communities.

**Pass =** the relaunch beats your last launch's signups or revenue.

**Next steps:**
1. Bundle recent work into one "big" feature.
2. Tease it, then drop it with a demo.
3. Compare to the prior launch.

**Decision:** beats it → make relaunches a monthly rhythm. Flat → space them out; you're launching too often.

> *Real example — Alex Finn, Creator Buddy: months of build-in-public, then $100K ARR on launch day.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools:** the launch venues themselves — Product Hunt, AppSumo/RocketHub, Hacker News — need no extra software.
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** **Claude in Chrome** to prep assets and monitor the launch and first comments; the **Notion** MCP for the launch checklist and asset kit; a **scheduled task** for the launch-day timeline. Check the registry for a Stripe/analytics MCP to watch the spike convert.

## 7. Outreach

#### First-Line Personalization Test
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** High · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** A personalized first line about [the prospect] will beat a generic opener on reply rate.

**The test:** same offer, two openers (personalized vs generic), 50 sends each.

**Pass =** the personalized opener clears 2× the reply rate, within 1 week.

**Next steps:**
1. Build a 100-prospect list.
2. Write a first line referencing something real about each.
3. Split 50/50; compare replies.

**Decision:** wins → personalize all sends (or automate the research). Flat → the offer, not the opener, is weak.

> *Real example — Marcos, Birdhouse, $65K/mo: disciplined, personalized DMs on Twitter.*

#### Offer Test
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** High · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Leading with [a sharper offer or outcome] will book more calls than our current pitch.

**The test:** test 2–3 value props in the opener across matched batches.

**Pass =** one offer books 2× the calls or replies, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Write 3 one-line offers, each a different outcome.
2. Send each to 30–50 matched prospects.
3. Track replies and calls per offer.

**Decision:** a winner → make it your standard pitch. None → revisit who you're targeting.

> *Real example — Brian Shin, Once, $20K/mo in 83 days: DM'd 300 via hashtags with a tight commitment ask.*

#### Follow-Up Sequence
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** Medium · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Adding 2–3 follow-ups to non-responders will lift total replies without new prospects.

**The test:** add a 3-touch follow-up over 7 days to everyone who didn't reply.

**Pass =** the follow-ups add 50%+ more replies on the same list.

**Next steps:**
1. Write 3 short follow-ups (bump, value, breakup).
2. Send to non-responders on days 2, 4, and 7.
3. Count the added replies.

**Decision:** lifts → bake follow-ups into every sequence. Flat → the list or offer is the issue, not persistence.

> *Real example — Andre, ListKit, $200K MRR: cold email at scale with disciplined sequences.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools** ([BONUS - AI Distribution Tools](BONUS-AI-Distribution-Tools.md) §4): **Apollo.io** for data + sequencing, **Clay** for enrichment and first-line personalization (First-Line Test), **Lemlist**/**Instantly**/**Smartlead** for sending and follow-ups.
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** the **Apollo plugin** is the standout — `prospect` → `enrich-lead` → `sequence-load` runs the whole find-enrich-sequence loop natively. Pair with **Claude in Chrome** to research prospects and a CRM MCP to track replies → calls.

## 8. Influencer / creator

#### Micro-Creator Batch
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** Seeding 5–10 micro-creators whose audience is our customer will produce a profitable cost-per-install.

**The test:** brief 5–10 micro-creators on the same demo; track installs per creator.

**Pass =** at least one creator's cost-per-install lands below your payback threshold.

**Next steps:**
1. List creators whose audience is your customer.
2. Offer a small flat fee (~$100–150) plus free access.
3. Track installs per creator with a code or link.

**Decision:** a profitable creator → re-run and find lookalikes. None → audience mismatch; re-pick creators.

> *Real example — Kelechi, Social Wizard, $60K/mo: paid micro-streamers ~$120/video to demo to exactly his demographic.*

#### Brief/Angle Test
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** Giving creators [a specific angle] will convert better than letting them freestyle.

**The test:** give creators 2–3 different hooks/angles; compare conversion.

**Pass =** one angle converts 2× the others across comparable creators.

**Next steps:**
1. Write 2–3 angles in your customer's words.
2. Assign them across creators.
3. Compare installs/sales per angle.

**Decision:** a winning angle → put it in every brief. Flat → let top creators freestyle; they know their audience.

> *Real example — Blake Anderson, Cal AI, $1M+/mo: scaled on influencer + UGC showing the magic moment.*

#### Boost the Winner
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** Putting ad spend behind the best organic creator post will scale it profitably.

**The test:** take the top-performing creator video and run it as a (whitelisted) paid ad to a matched audience.

**Pass =** the boosted post keeps cost-per-install under payback at 3× the organic spend.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick the creator post with the best organic conversion.
2. Get usage rights; run it as an ad.
3. Scale budget only while cost-per-install holds.

**Decision:** holds → scale spend stepwise. Breaks → return to organic seeding.

> *Real example — QALO Ring: influencer seeding (Andy Dalton) scaled into a national brand.*

1. List operators whose audience trusts them.

3. Give them a link and assets; track sales.

**Decision:** producing → recruit more like the top partner. Flat → commission or fit is off.

> *Real example — Ivan, Lancer, $10K/mo in 3–4 months: grew via connector/affiliate partnerships, no paid ads.*

## 10. Email & lifecycle

#### Welcome Series
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** A short triggered welcome series that walks new signups to [your magic moment] will activate more of them than dropping them in cold.

**The test:** build a 3-email sequence on signup — the quick win, the next step, the proof/upgrade — each deep-linked into the product.

**Pass =** new signups in the series reach the magic moment at a rate clearly above the no-email baseline, within 30 days.

**Next steps:**
1. Write 3 short emails, each driving one action toward the magic moment.
2. Trigger them on signup (day 0 / 1 / 3) in your email tool.
3. Compare activation for signups with vs. without the series.

**Decision:** lifts activation → make it the default and add a fourth email. Flat → the friction is in-product, not email (run the activation & retention audit).

> *Real example — Dustin, Magai, ~$100K/mo: a 100K-subscriber email list is the engine that converts and re-sells across his products — the owned audience does the selling.*

#### Newsletter Broadcast
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** A regular, genuinely useful broadcast will keep your list warm and drive repeat visits back to [your product].

**The test:** send a consistent newsletter or product update (weekly or biweekly) that gives subscribers a reason to keep opening and clicking.

**Pass =** a healthy open/click rate holds across 4 sends, and broadcasts drive a measurable bump in return visits or conversions.

**Next steps:**
1. Pick a cadence you can sustain and one useful angle per send.
2. Write each issue to end with one deep link back to the product.
3. Track opens, clicks, and the return visits each send drives.

**Decision:** holds attention and drives clicks → lock the cadence and grow the list. Decays → tighten the value or cut the frequency.

> *Real example — Alex Garcia, Marketing Examined: grew a newsletter from ~2K to 8–9K subscribers into a ~$800K/year business on the strength of a regular, genuinely useful broadcast.*

#### Win-Back Sequence
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** Medium · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** A short triggered sequence to dormant users will reactivate a chunk of them for almost no cost.

**The test:** fire a 2–3 email win-back to anyone inactive for [N] days — "you left X unfinished," the reason to return, a final nudge.

**Pass =** the sequence reactivates a meaningful share of dormant users (e.g. 5–10%) within 30 days.

**Next steps:**
1. Define "dormant" (e.g. no login in 14–30 days) and trigger on it.
2. Write 2–3 emails: the unfinished value, a reason to return, a final nudge.
3. Measure reactivations against the dormant cohort that got nothing.

**Decision:** reactivates → make it permanent and test offers. Flat → the churn is value-driven; fix the product, not the email.

> *Real example — Alex Lieberman, Morning Brew, $75M+/yr: the whole model is daily re-engagement — getting readers to open again tomorrow — proving the owned inbox is the cheapest place to win attention back.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools** ([BONUS - AI Distribution Tools](BONUS-AI-Distribution-Tools.md) §7): **Loops** for SaaS lifecycle email (Welcome Series, Win-Back), **Customer.io** for behavioral triggers, **Beehiiv** for a newsletter (Newsletter Broadcast), **Klaviyo** for e-commerce.
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** the **Notion** MCP to run the email calendar and draft sequences; a **scheduled task** to hold your broadcast cadence; **Claude in Chrome** + **web search** to research subject lines and competitor newsletters. Check the connector registry for your ESP MCP (Loops, Klaviyo, Mailchimp) and a Stripe MCP to tie email to revenue.

## 12. Ads

#### Validation Test
**Effort:** Low · **Impact:** High · ⭐ *quick win*

> **Hypothesis:** Running several plain, text-only image ads — each stating a different version of [your product offer] — will reveal which promise the market actually responds to, cheaply, before you invest in real creative or scale any channel.

**The test:** make 4–6 simple image ads, each showing just one version of the offer as text (a different headline or angle on each), and run them to one tight audience at a small daily budget, all driving to your landing page or app store listing.

**Pass =** one offer ad clears a click-through rate clearly above the rest (≈2×) and drives a meaningful number of clicks to the page/listing on a small budget (e.g. ~$50–100 over 5–7 days).

**Next steps:**
1. Write 4–6 one-line versions of your offer — each a different outcome, pain, or hook, in the customer's words.
2. Put each on a plain image (just the text, no design polish); set up one ad per version to one tight audience.
3. Point every ad at your landing page or app store listing; set a small daily budget.
4. After 5–7 days, compare click-through (and signups/installs) per version; keep the winner and cut the rest.

**Decision:** a clear winner → make that line your headline everywhere (landing page, listing, organic hooks) and pour creative behind it (Creative Volume Test). Nothing gets clicks → the offer itself isn't landing — revisit `productos/define/1-Product-Offer.md` before spending more.

> *Real example — Nico, Make Logo AI, ~$85K in 6 months: ran static image ads into audience-specific landing pages, letting the ad that won clicks reveal which message and audience to pour spend into.*

#### Creative Volume Test
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** Many variations of our best organic winner will find a profitable paid creative.

**The test:** turn the top organic post into 5–10 ad variations aimed at one tight audience.

**Pass =** at least one creative holds cost-per-acquisition under payback, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Take your best-converting organic content.
2. Cut 5–10 variations (hooks, thumbnails).
3. Run them to one audience; kill losers fast.

**Decision:** a profitable creative → scale it and make more like it. None → the organic hook didn't transfer; don't widen spend.

> *Real example — Steven Cravada, PuffCount, $40K/mo: turned on TikTok/Meta ads only after organic proved the hook.*

#### Audience Test
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** Medium

> **Hypothesis:** Our winning creative will perform better against [a specific audience] than broad targeting.

**The test:** run the same creative across 3–5 tightly-defined audiences.

**Pass =** one audience beats the others on cost-per-acquisition by 30%+.

**Next steps:**
1. Define 3–5 narrow audiences.
2. Run the winning creative to each at small budget.
3. Compare cost-per-acquisition; scale the winner.

**Decision:** a winner → concentrate spend there. All weak → the offer or landing page, not the audience.

> *Real example — Nico, Make Logo AI, ~$85K in 6 months: Facebook static ads into audience-specific landing pages.*

#### Landing-Page Match
**Effort:** Medium · **Impact:** High · *big bet*

> **Hypothesis:** A dedicated landing page matched to the ad will convert better than the homepage.

**The test:** send one ad's traffic to a tailored landing page versus the homepage.

**Pass =** the matched page lifts ad-to-signup 2×, within 2 weeks.

**Next steps:**
1. Build a page that mirrors the ad's promise.
2. Split traffic between the page and the homepage.
3. Compare signup rate.

**Decision:** lifts → build a page per ad angle. Flat → the gap is the offer; fix that first.

> *Real example — Nico, Make Logo AI: audience-specific landing pages were central to the paid scale-up.*

**Recommended tools & plugins**

- **Tools** ([BONUS - AI Distribution Tools](BONUS-AI-Distribution-Tools.md) §5): **AdCreative.ai** for fast static offer ads (Validation Test, Creative Volume Test), **Arcads**/**Creatify** for UGC video, **Foreplay** for ad-swipe research, **Madgicx** for management (Audience Test), **HeyGen** for localization.
- **Automate in Claude/Codex:** the **Figma** plugin to produce the text-on-image offer ads (Validation Test) and creative variations; **Claude in Chrome** for ad-swipe research. Check the registry for a Meta/Google Ads MCP to launch and read results, and an analytics/Stripe MCP for cost-per-acquisition.
